## Fifteen years of features, stacked in the order they were built

The legacy profile was overwhelming. Every feature added over 15 years got its own box with the same visual weight: stats, About Me, expertise, transcripts and certificates, resume, calendar integration, followers, interests and administered topics. Nothing was arranged around why someone opened a profile, so the answer they came for was buried somewhere in the stack.

## What do people actually open a profile for?

We prioritized the features our users were looking for in profiles. With Knowledge Coop's new mission to build up teams through community, community features earned a place near the top too. That gave the redesign five jobs:

- **Show relevant information first.** Location, birthday, role and a short bio sit next to the photo, so you know who you're talking to in a second.
- **Build teams up through community.** The profile shows the communities a person belongs to, and the ones you have in common.
- **Make certificates and transcripts easy to reach.** They're one click away instead of buried in a box of links.
- **Show what people own.** The portfolio lists the content a person owns or collaborates on.
- **Show what someone is doing, at a glance.** Recent activity summarizes their latest comments, answers, courses and posts.

## Building teams up, not just listing people

Community is how the intranet turns a directory into a team. When you visit someone's profile, a line under their bio tells you what you share: "You're both in Design Guild and Vancouver Office." It's a built-in conversation starter. The Communities column shows where the person spends time, with member counts and whether each community is public or private. From someone else's profile you can invite them to a community. From your own, you can explore new ones.

---

## See what someone is working on without leaving their profile

Recent activity replaced the legacy feed's mix of thumbnails and truncated posts with one clear line per action: commented on, completed, answered, reacted to, published. Hovering an item previews the content it's tied to, so you can see what a comment was about without leaving the profile. A filter narrows the feed by type when you're looking for something specific.

## Your profile, your controls

The same layout adapts when you're on your own profile. Edit profile sits at the top. Certificates gain an Upload action, and your transcript is one click away. The portfolio splits into content you can edit and content you can only view.
